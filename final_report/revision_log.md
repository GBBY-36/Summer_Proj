# Revision Log

## Scope

This revision updates both the Chinese and English versions of the AML candidate-target report in `final_report/`. The original analytical outputs were not changed: sample sizes, gene counts, candidate gene lists, HR values, confidence intervals, P values, DLBCL results, graph ranks, cosine similarities, and figure data remain unchanged.

## Detailed Changes

1. Candidate interpretation for IL1R2, SLC15A3, and VNN2
   - Original issue: the three main candidates could be read as if they had demonstrated AML-over-normal therapeutic expression windows.
   - Revised text: both versions now explicitly state that IL1R2, SLC15A3, and VNN2 are all down-regulated in the AML-versus-normal comparison, and that their current priority comes mainly from AML-internal survival association and network-assisted evidence.
   - Reason: avoids implying surface-target or therapeutic-window evidence that was not generated by the project.

2. Candidate table structure
   - Original issue: a dense candidate table mixed evidence ranking, localization, intervention logic, and experimental priority.
   - Revised text: the table was split into two tables: an evidence table and a translational-validation table. The column formerly framed as experimental priority was revised to `Suggested next validation focus` / `建议的下一步验证重点`.
   - Reason: improves readability and avoids assigning high/medium priority scores that were not part of the original analysis.

3. AML and DLBCL HR comparability
   - Original issue: AML and DLBCL hazard ratios could be overread as directly equivalent.
   - Revised text: Figure 2 captions now state that both cohorts use cohort-specific median splits, AML uses univariate Cox, and DLBCL uses treatment-adjusted Cox. The figure is framed as a comparison of direction, confidence intervals, and cross-disease patterns rather than strict HR magnitude equivalence.
   - Reason: clarifies model differences without changing any HR or P values.

4. HSPC filtering definition
   - Original issue: the log2 expression cutoff of 7.0 needed clearer operational definition.
   - Revised text: methods now specify that GSE24759 was read as a normalized microarray matrix, GPL570 annotation mapped probes to genes, duplicate probes were averaged, HSC/CMP/GMP/MEP samples were selected from sample titles, and each gene was filtered by mean expression across selected HSPC samples. Genes with `hspc_mean_expr >= 7.0` were marked HSPC-high and excluded.
   - Reason: makes the filtering rule reproducible and states that 7.0 is an internal operational threshold, not a universal safety boundary.

5. Figure 1 title and caption
   - Original issue: the previous internal title, `Forest Plot of Prognostic Hazard Genes in AML`, sounded too confirmatory.
   - Revised figure: `Exploratory Univariate Cox Results for AML Candidate Genes`; subtitle: `HR > 1.5 and nominal P < 0.05`.
   - Reason: aligns the figure with exploratory candidate prioritization.

6. Figure 3 title and caption
   - Original issue: `UMAP Proximity` could imply that two-dimensional visual distance was formal evidence.
   - Revised figure: `UMAP Visualization of Node2Vec Embeddings for AML Target Candidates`.
   - Revised caption: both versions state that UMAP is visualization only, two-dimensional projection may distort high-dimensional distances, and candidate ranking is based on cosine similarity in the original 128-dimensional Node2Vec space.
   - Reason: separates 128-dimensional ranking evidence from two-dimensional visualization.

7. Network limitation
   - Original issue: network-assisted evidence needed clearer limitations.
   - Revised text: limitations now state that network proximity cannot measure drug response, gene dependency, causal mechanism, surface accessibility, or normal-tissue safety, and that ranking depends on 14 AML target seeds, node degree, and heterogeneous STRING/CTD evidence.
   - Reason: prevents overinterpreting network similarity as therapeutic mechanism.

8. Project significance and conclusion
   - Original issue: the conclusion repeated results and did not fully foreground evidence boundaries.
   - Revised text: the conclusion now frames the workflow as independent evidence layers: cross-cohort expression, HSPC filtering, AML survival association, DLBCL outcome comparison, and network context. IL1R2, SLC15A3, and VNN2 are described as experimental priorities, not therapeutic claims.
   - Reason: improves scientific rigor and formal tone.

9. Layout and pagination
   - Original issue: the English candidate table split awkwardly across pages, and the conclusion/reference transition needed better control.
   - Revised layout: the English translational-validation table now starts cleanly on a new page; both reports are 6 pages. The conclusion is complete on page 5 and references begin on page 6 in both PDF outputs.
   - Reason: improves report readability while keeping the report within the requested 4-6 page range.

10. Chinese PDF pagination support
    - Original issue: the custom Chinese PDF generator did not interpret Word page breaks, so references could begin on the wrong page.
    - Revised code: `build_final_cn_pdf.py` now detects Word page breaks and emits ReportLab `PageBreak`.
    - Reason: keeps DOCX and PDF pagination aligned.

11. Removed unused temporary scripts
    - Original issue: two failed figure-regeneration helper scripts were left in `scripts/`.
    - Revised files: removed `scripts/regenerate_report_figures.py` and `scripts/update_report_figure_titles.py`.
    - Reason: avoids confusing those failed experiments with the final reproducible report workflow.

## Items Not Recomputed

The following items were intentionally kept as limitations or future work. No new values were generated:

- BH-FDR correction for the 106-gene survival screen.
- Continuous standardized-expression Cox models.
- Multivariable AML Cox models including age, cytogenetic risk, mutation status, treatment, or ELN risk group.
- Independent AML survival replication.
- New cosine-similarity ranking plots beyond the existing Node2Vec rank/cosine table and UMAP visualization.
